- The link redirects through multiple shorteners before reaching Discord
- The server immediately asks for your Roblox credentials, Robux, or a "verification" purchase
- There are no moderators online and no message history older than a few days
- The server name is a near-miss spelling of the game

## First-Week Playbook for New Members

Jumping into a large, difficulty-focused server can be intimidating. Here's a sequence that works, drawn from common community advice.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the rules and pinned messages in each channel | Prevents accidental mutes and repeated questions |
| 2 | Set your roles if the server offers them | Unlocks tower-specific channels and pings |
| 3 | Search before posting | Most beginner questions already have answers |
| 4 | Share a clip or a completion | Fastest way to become a familiar name |
| 5 | Ask specific questions | "How do I beat stage 7 of X" beats "help" |
| 6 | Give back once you improve | Communities reward reciprocity |

Beyond that, a few etiquette basics go a long way:

- Don't spoil a tower's ending or secret route without warning tags
- Post screenshots rather than long text descriptions of a bug
- Avoid tier-list arguments in general chat — there's usually a dedicated channel
- Credit the person who taught you a strategy

Players who follow this pattern generally report finding practice partners within a few days, which matters enormously in a game where solo grinding can stall out for weeks.
