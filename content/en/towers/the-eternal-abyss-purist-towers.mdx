## What Are Purist Towers in The Eternal Abyss?

### The core definition

In tower-obby communities, a **purist** tower is one built without client objects — with music zones as the only accepted exception. In plain terms, that means every platform, killbrick, conveyor, and obstacle you interact with is standard, server-side geometry. Nothing you step on depends on a scripted prop that exists only on your own machine.

For The Eternal Abyss, this has real gameplay consequences. A purist tower cannot surprise you with a moving part that desyncs mid-jump, and it cannot hide a shortcut behind a custom object that only loads on some clients. What you see in a full run video is what you get when you press play.

### Where the term comes from

"Purism" is shared vocabulary across the wider tower-obby scene, and it shows up in wikis for multiple games in the genre, including Juke's Towers of Hell. Community editors use it as a quality label: it tells players that a tower's difficulty comes from level design and timing rather than from scripts. The Eternal Abyss follows the same convention, and its wiki sorts qualifying towers into a dedicated category.

### Why the distinction is not just cosmetic

Client objects are not inherently bad. They allow flashier set pieces, custom animations, and creative hazards. But they also introduce variables a player cannot control: server latency, client performance, and the occasional desync. Purism removes those variables, which is exactly why veteran players treat it as a mark of a "clean" tower.

| Feature | Purist tower | Tower using client objects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client-side props | None (music zones excepted) | One or more |
| Source of difficulty | Platforming, timing, route knowledge | Mechanics, scripts, moving parts |
| Run consistency | High — identical every attempt | Can vary by client or server state |
| Latency sensitivity | Lower | Often higher |
| Community perception | Frequently seen as "pure" or "classic" | Judged case by case |

## FAQ

**What exactly makes a tower purist in The Eternal Abyss?**
A tower is considered purist when it uses no client objects, with music zones excluded from that rule. In practice, that means every obstacle you interact with is standard server-side geometry, so the challenge comes from level design rather than from scripts.

**Are the eternal abyss purist towers harder than other towers?**
Not automatically, but many of the toughest builds in the game happen to be purist. Because the difficulty comes purely from timing and precision, purist towers tend to punish weak fundamentals more directly than towers that lean on client-side mechanics.

**Do music zones disqualify a tower from the purist category?**
No. Music zones are the accepted exception in the standard definition, so a tower can still be listed as purist even if it plays custom audio in certain sections.

**How can I find more purist towers to play?**
Start with the community wiki's Purist category, then cross-check against recent full-clear videos to confirm a tower still qualifies after updates. Community reports are the fastest source, but always verify in-game before committing to a long grind.

The eternal abyss purist towers reward patience more than raw reflexes. Pick one from the purist list, learn it in sections, and let consistency do the rest.
