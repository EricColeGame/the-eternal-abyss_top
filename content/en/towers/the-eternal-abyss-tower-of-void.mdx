If you have spent any time climbing in The Eternal Abyss, you have heard of the Tower of Void — and you have probably gone looking for a straight answer about it. The Eternal Abyss Tower of Void is one of the most talked-about challenges in the game: a long, punishing climb that mixes precision jumps with tight timing and almost no tolerance for sloppy camera work. That is exactly why so many players hunt down an Eternal Abyss Tower of Void walkthrough before they even touch the first platform. This guide explains what the tower actually is, how it differs from its similarly named cousins, and the route and movement habits that give you a real shot at reaching the top.

## What Is the Tower of Void in The Eternal Abyss?

The Tower of Void — usually shortened to **ToV** by players — is a void-themed tower that community wiki entries connect directly to the Citadel of Void. Instead of being a fully standalone build, it is described as a slightly toned-down version of the first ten floors of that citadel: the same general layout, the same core ideas, but with adjustments that make a clear run realistically possible for a skilled climber.

That relationship is the single most important thing to understand about the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void. If you have already studied citadel routes, you are not starting from zero. If you have not, you are effectively learning a compressed and somewhat more forgiving slice of some of the hardest content in the game — which is still an enormous task.

### The three "void" towers players constantly mix up

Community reports describe the **Tower of Eternal Void** (ToEV) as a Horrific-difficulty, mixed-progression tower located in the Pit of Misery, credited to a builder known as Karinriine. It shares a name, a theme, and a floor concept with the Tower of Void, but it is a separate challenge with its own placement and its own reputation. Assuming they are the same build is one of the most common mistakes new climbers make.

**Table 1: How the void towers relate to each other (community-reported)**

| Tower | Common abbreviation | Where it sits | Difficulty reputation | Relationship to the Citadel of Void |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tower of Void | ToV | Void-themed tower slot | Very high — often called the hardest tower of its type | A slightly nerfed version of the citadel's first ten floors |
| Tower of Eternal Void | ToEV | Pit of Misery | Horrific, mixed progression | A nerfed take on the same ten-floor concept |
| Citadel of Void | CoV | Citadel slot | The original, un-nerfed source | The full build both towers borrow from |

One thing worth flagging: community chatter has occasionally suggested the Tower of Void could be removed or reworked at some point. Treat that as **unverified player talk**, not a confirmed schedule — but it is a fair reason to attempt it sooner rather than later.

## Difficulty and Length: What the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void Demands

Because the tower reuses citadel concepts, its difficulty is not really about any single insane jump. It is about sustained precision across ten floors with very little forgiveness. Players who clear it consistently tend to describe the same handful of pressure points.

**Table 2: The real skill demands of the Tower of Void**

| Skill area | Why it decides your run | How to train it |
|---|---|---|
| Camera and shift-lock control | Most falls come from bad angles, not bad jumps | Practice wraparound walls with shift lock on and off |
| Jump timing on moving parts | A single mistimed cycle costs the whole section | Watch one full cycle before you move |
| Corner and edge precision | Small platforms punish over-committing | Do low-risk edge jumps in easier towers first |
| Endurance between checkpoints | Fatigue causes late-floor mistakes | Take a 30-second pause at every checkpoint |
| Composure after a wipe | Tilt leads to repeat failures | Reset your camera and breathe before retrying |

If you have cleared high-end towers before, expect the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void to feel like a step up rather than a wall. If this is your first serious void attempt, expect several sessions before your first real clear.

### Is it harder than the Citadel of Void?

Almost certainly not, and that is by design. The whole point of a nerfed version is to make the same ideas survivable. What makes the Tower of Void feel brutal is not raw difficulty — it is the *density*. Ten floors of citadel-grade design back to back gives you very few breather sections.

## Route Strategy: Breaking the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void Into Stages

The biggest mistake players make is treating the tower as one continuous test. Treat it as ten separate problems instead, and your clear rate improves fast.

### Step 1: Recon before you commit

Load in and use free camera or a safe vantage point to look at the first few floors before you start jumping. Note where the killbricks sit, which platforms move, and which jumps require you to wait for a cycle. Two minutes of looking saves twenty minutes of dying.

### Step 2: Solve floors in pairs

Group the ten floors into five pairs. Learn the first pair until you can clear it without thinking, then move your practice focus to the next pair. This prevents the classic trap of being excellent at floor one and hopeless at floor seven.

**Table 3: Pre-run preparation checklist**

| Step | What to do | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set your camera sensitivity before your first jump | Mid-run changes wreck muscle memory |
| 2 | Scout floors 1–3 from a safe position | You learn cycles without burning attempts |
| 3 | Identify your personal "hard floor" | You know where to slow down |
| 4 | Pick a consistent checkpoint discipline | Prevents panic-retrying after a fall |
| 5 | Warm up in an easier tower first | Hands and timing need a ramp-up |

### Step 3: Build a personal route map

Write down, in your own words, what each floor asks of you: "wraparound, then two timed jumps, then a truss climb." Community route videos are useful, but the notes you write yourself are what you will actually remember under pressure.

## Movement, Camera, and Checkpoint Tactics

Mechanics matter more than route knowledge at the top end. Most wipes on the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void happen because a player knows exactly what to do and still mistimes the input.

**Table 4: Common mistakes and their fixes**

| Mistake | What it costs you | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Jumping the instant you land | You drift off-line and miss the next platform | Land, settle, then jump |
| Fighting the camera mid-jump | You lose your reference point | Set the angle before you leave the ground |
| Sprinting everywhere | Overshoot on small platforms | Walk on precision sections |
| Retrying instantly after a fall | You repeat the same error | Pause, re-scout, then retry |
| Ignoring easy floors | A careless early fall ends a good run | Play floor one like floor ten |

### Checkpoint discipline

Checkpoints are your pacing tool, not just a safety net. Players who clear long towers tend to do three things at every checkpoint: reset the camera, check the next section from a standstill, and consciously relax their grip. It sounds trivial, but community reports consistently tie late-run wipes to tension and rushed inputs rather than impossible jumps.

## Practice Routine, Community Reports, and Honest Expectations

Improvement in a tower like this is not linear. You will have sessions where you reach floor eight and sessions where floor two eats you alive. A structured routine smooths that out.

**Table 5: A simple weekly practice plan**

| Session focus | Goal | Suggested length |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Shake off rust in an easier tower | 10–15 minutes |
| Route review | Re-scout your two weakest floors | 15 minutes |
| Serious attempts | Full runs with no resets | 30–45 minutes |
| Review | Note what actually killed you | 5 minutes |

A few honest expectations, based on player experience shared across the community:

- **Your first clear will probably not be clean.** Finishing is the goal; style comes later.
- **Progress comes in plateaus.** You may stall on one floor for days, then pass three floors in one session.
- **Community videos help, but they are not gospel.** Different movement styles suit different players.
- **Nerfed does not mean easy.** A toned-down citadel floor is still a citadel floor.

## FAQ

**Is the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void the same as the Tower of Eternal Void?**
No. They are separate builds that share a void theme and a ten-floor concept. Community wiki entries place the Tower of Eternal Void in the Pit of Misery at Horrific difficulty, while the Tower of Void is treated as a slightly nerfed version of the Citadel of Void's opening ten floors.

**How hard is the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void compared to the Citadel of Void?**
By design it is more forgiving — it is described as a toned-down take on the same floors. It still ranks among the hardest towers of its type, so do not expect a casual clear.

**What is the biggest reason players fail the Tower of Void?**
Inconsistent camera control and instant retries. Most falls are caused by bad angles and rushed inputs, not by jumps that are genuinely impossible.

**Should I practice inside the tower or somewhere easier first?**
Use easier towers to build the specific skills you need — edge jumps, truss climbs, timed cycles — then bring those skills into the Eternal Abyss Tower of Void for full attempts. Practicing only inside the tower tends to teach you the layout without fixing the underlying mistakes.
