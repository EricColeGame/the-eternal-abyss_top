## What Counts as the Eternal Abyss Easiest Tower?

The straightforward answer: **the Eternal Abyss easiest tower is any tower sitting in the Easy difficulty tier.** Easy is a defined difficulty in the game, and the design team spread those towers out on purpose — each area features exactly one Easy tower, and there are currently four Easy towers in-game across the game's four available areas.

That structure is the whole point. Instead of dumping every beginner-friendly climb into one hub, the game gives you a single low-difficulty target per area. You always have somewhere to go when the harder towers in that zone start wrecking you.

What Easy does *not* mean is "trivial." Community reports from newer players consistently describe Easy towers as forgiving but not free — they teach core mechanics like timing, camera control, and checkpoint management without punishing a single slip. Treat them as tutorials with teeth.

## Why the Easy Tier Exists (and Why It Isn't an Insult)

The Eternal Abyss is built on a difficulty chart philosophy — a visible ladder of tiers so players always know what they're signing up for. That approach traces back to the Tower of Difficulty Chart, which is credited with starting the difficulty chart trend and inspiring later towers such as Tower of Ease to Abyss and Tower of Adversity.

The practical upshot for you: the game tells you the difficulty before you commit. You don't have to guess whether a tower is a warm-up or a wall. If you're brand new, that label is your best friend, not a slight.

| Tower | Known for | Beginner relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Tower of Difficulty Chart | Started the difficulty chart trend | Shows how the game signals difficulty up front |
| Tower of Ease to Abyss | Built as a low-difficulty entry point | A reasonable early benchmark for new players |
| Tower of Adversity | A harder chart-inspired climb | Save it until you've cleared several Easy towers |

A quick note on framing: "easiest" is relative to *you*. If you're comfortable with tight platforming but struggle with long climbs, a short mid-tier tower might feel easier than a long Easy one. Use the tier labels as your starting filter, then adjust based on how your first attempt actually goes.

## Step-by-Step: Finding and Clearing the Eternal Abyss Easiest Tower

Here's a repeatable routine you can run in every new area. It takes a few minutes and saves a lot of deaths.

1. **Enter the area and orient yourself.** Head toward the green front end of the zone rather than the purple forest side.
2. **Identify the Easy tower.** There is one per area — scan for the tower marked Easy on the difficulty chart before climbing anything.
3. **Do a scouting run.** Climb as far as you can without pressure. You're learning the layout, not going for the clear.
4. **Mark the hard sections mentally.** Note the two or three jumps that killed your scouting run. Those are your practice targets.
5. **Run it for real.** Now climb with intent, treating each checkpoint as a mini-goal.
6. **Clear it, then repeat it.** A second clear on the same tower builds the muscle memory that carries into mid-tier climbs.

| Step | Action | Why it works | Time needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Locate the green front end | Skips the hardest zone entirely | Under a minute |
| 2 | Find the Easy-marked tower | Confirms you're on the right climb | Under a minute |
| 3 | Scouting run | Removes pressure, builds a mental map | A few minutes |
| 4 | Note the failure points | Turns vague difficulty into specific practice | A few minutes |
| 5 | Intentional clear attempt | Focused effort after preparation | Varies |
| 6 | Repeat clear | Converts a fluke into real skill | Varies |

One more tip from community reports: if a specific jump in an Easy tower has failed you more than five times, don't grind it blindly. Step back, watch the platform's rhythm, and approach it from a different camera angle. Most "impossible" Easy jumps are camera problems, not skill problems.

Tower order matters more than raw talent in the early game. The official guide hub for the game recommends a beginner tower order built around the lowest-difficulty climbs first — a solid blueprint to follow if you want a structured path instead of wandering.

## Mistakes That Make Easy Towers Feel Harder Than They Are

Most players who insist "Easy towers are actually hard" are making one of these errors:

- **Starting in the purple forest.** The Unreal zone is not a shortcut to the Easy towers. Check the color of the terrain before you climb.
- **Skipping the scouting run.** Diving straight into a clear attempt means every mistake is a surprise instead of a known obstacle.
- **Ignoring the difficulty chart.** The tiers exist so you can pick a fight you can win. Guessing wastes time.
- **Grinding the same failed jump.** Five failures means change your approach, not your effort level.
- **Skipping the repeat clear.** One clear proves you got lucky once. Two clears prove you learned something.
- **Comparing yourself to speedrunners.** Community reports show that experienced players clear Easy towers in a fraction of the time a beginner needs. That gap is practice, not talent.

Fix the first three and the Eternal Abyss easiest tower stops being a question and starts being a routine.
