## How to Prepare for the Hardest Towers

Preparation matters more than raw talent at this level. Players who clear top-tier content consistently tend to follow a similar routine rather than grinding blindly.

| Step | What To Do | Why It Helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm up | Clear a mid-tier tower first | Builds timing rhythm before precision work |
| 2. Scout | Watch a full clear before attempting | Removes surprise deaths from unknown sections |
| 3. Segment | Practice the hardest section in isolation | Turns a 20-minute run into a 2-minute drill |
| 4. Reset clean | Restart immediately after a bad death | Prevents tilt from compounding |
| 5. Log failures | Note where you died each attempt | Reveals patterns instead of random bad luck |

Bullet-point habits that separate consistent clearers from stuck players:

- **Practice the ending first.** Most wipes happen in the final third, where nerves and fatigue peak.
- **Cap your sessions.** Long grinds degrade precision; short, focused attempts beat marathon runs.
- **Change one variable at a time.** Adjusting your camera, movement, or timing all at once makes it impossible to learn what worked.
- **Treat checkpoints as data.** Every checkpoint reached is evidence that a section is solved.

The players who eventually conquer the Eternal Abyss hardest tower are usually not the fastest learners — they are the ones who fail in a structured way.

## Mistakes That End Runs Early

| Mistake | What It Looks Like | The Fix |

The last point deserves emphasis. Jumping straight from Insane-tier content into the hardest listed tower is the most common way players quit. The chart exists to be climbed in sequence, and the average difficulty of Mid Catastrophic tells you exactly how steep that sequence gets.
