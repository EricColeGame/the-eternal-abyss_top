## What Is The Eternal Abyss?

The Eternal Abyss is a Roblox tower-climbing game built on a simple, brutal promise: it will not hold your hand. The official game description is unusually blunt about this, warning players that losing progress and getting frustrated are expected parts of the experience rather than bugs to be fixed. That design philosophy shapes everything about how you should approach it — including how you plan your tower order.

If you are new, the smartest thing you can do is stop treating the game as one giant climb and start treating it as a library of individual challenges. Each tower is a self-contained gauntlet, and each one has its own difficulty curve, its own tricks, and its own community reputation. Learning to read those signals early saves you dozens of hours.

Here is the high-level picture before we dig into the details.

| Attribute | What to Expect |
|---|---|
| Platform | Roblox |
| Genre | Tower obby / difficulty climbing |
| Handholding | Almost none — the game states this outright |
| Core unit of progress | Individual towers, each usually built around 10 floors |
| Standard failure state | Losing progress and restarting the climb |
| Community hubs | Fan wikis, tier list builders, and dedicated guide sites |

The game's own description frames frustration as a feature. That is not marketing spin — it is a genuine warning. Players who accept that framing from the start tend to stick with the game far longer than players who expect a smooth difficulty ramp.

## How the Tower System Works

Every tower in the game follows a loose shared structure. According to community wiki documentation, the standard tower spans roughly 10 floors, though the same source notes that developers occasionally release towers with more or fewer floors than the norm. That outlier rule matters: never assume a climb is "almost over" just because you have cleared nine floors.

Towers are also grouped geographically. Community-maintained wiki pages describe themed sections, such as a purple forest region that hosts a category of towers known as Unreal towers. Other towers sit in more isolated positions — the Tower of Naive Facades, for example, is documented as standing alone on a tall, stalagmite-like pillar adjacent to the main island of towers. That isolation is often a hint that the climb is meant to be a milestone rather than a warm-up.

| Term | What It Refers To |
|---|---|
| Floor | A single stage within a tower; most towers have about 10 |
| Tower | A complete climb, start to finish, usually with a badge reward |
| Realm / Area | A themed region that groups multiple towers together |
| Unreal Tower | A tower category located in the purple forest section, per wiki documentation |
| Standalone Tower | A tower placed away from the main cluster, often for pacing reasons |
| Badge | The proof-of-completion reward attached to a tower |

The naming convention is worth internalizing too. Nearly every tower is titled "Tower of [Something]," and the name frequently telegraphs the gimmick. A name that sounds ominous is usually a warning about precision timing, tight jumps, or a mechanic that punishes hesitation.

## Difficulty Tiers: Reading the Labels Correctly

The game does not hand you a clean numeric difficulty score for every climb. Instead, the community does the ranking work. Tier list builders and fan wikis are the primary places where players sort towers from "reasonable first attempt" to "do not touch this yet." A popular community tier list template exists specifically for ranking every tower in the game.

Because these rankings are community-driven, treat them as strong guidance rather than gospel. Player experience varies wildly depending on input device, ping, and how comfortable you are with tight platforming.

| Community Tier | What It Usually Signals | Who Should Attempt It |
|---|---|---|
| Entry / Low | Forgiving jumps, generous checkpoints, simple gimmicks | New players still learning the controls |
| Mid | Requires consistent timing and some patience | Players with a handful of completions |
| High | Punishing sections, long stretches between checkpoints | Confident players with a solid completion count |
| Extreme | Precision-heavy, often with one-shot mechanics | Dedicated players grinding specific towers |
| Unranked / New | Too recently added for a settled community verdict | Anyone willing to be a test subject |

A useful rule of thumb: if a tower appears in the "trending pages" section of a fan wiki, it is probably either brand new, unusually hard, or both. Trending towers like the Tower of Inevitable Failure tend to draw attention precisely because they are causing players trouble.

## A Practical Progression Roadmap

The single biggest mistake new players make is attempting towers in the order they happen to encounter them. The second biggest mistake is attempting towers in the order they appear on a tier list, top to bottom. Neither approach accounts for your actual skill set.

Use this sequence instead.

| Step | Action | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete two or three entry-tier towers first | Builds muscle memory for the game's jump physics |
| 2 | Record which mechanics you fail most often | Turns vague frustration into a specific practice target |
| 3 | Move to mid-tier towers that use your weakest mechanic | Forces improvement instead of comfort grinding |
| 4 | Attempt one standalone tower as a milestone test | These are often placed deliberately as skill checks |
| 5 | Revisit any abandoned tower after a week | Fresh eyes fix problems repetition cannot |
| 6 | Only then start exploring higher tiers | Prevents burnout from repeated hard failures |

Steps two and five are the ones most players skip, and they are the ones that produce the fastest gains. Writing down "I keep missing the third jump in section two" is infinitely more useful than "this tower is impossible."

### Pacing Yourself Across Realms

Because towers are grouped into themed areas, it helps to clear a region's easier entries before pushing into its harder ones. This also gives you a natural stopping point: when a realm's remaining towers are all above your comfort level, that is your cue to move to a different region rather than bash your head against the same wall.

## Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most stalled progress in this game traces back to a handful of repeatable errors. The fixes are unglamorous but effective.

| Mistake | What It Costs You | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Attempting towers above your tier | Hours of repeated failure with little learning | Drop two tiers and rebuild fundamentals |
| Ignoring checkpoint behavior | Long replays of sections you already beat | Learn where checkpoints trigger before pushing forward |
| Playing while tilted | Sloppy inputs and worse decisions | Take a break; the game explicitly expects rage, so plan for it |
| Skipping the wiki entirely | Missing layout and category info that changes your route | Read the area page before you enter it |
| Never watching your own failures | Repeating the same error indefinitely | Note the exact section that kills you |
| Treating one tower as the whole game | Burnout | Rotate between two or three towers at different tiers |

The official Roblox listing for The Eternal Abyss is refreshingly honest that losing progress is part of the deal. Players who internalize that — and who build breaks into their sessions — consistently report a better time than players who grind one impossible climb for six straight hours.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many towers are in The Eternal Abyss?**

There is no fixed final number. Community wiki documentation notes that the developers' stated long-term goal is to reach around 50 towers in each area of the game, so the total grows as new regions are added. Check the wiki's tower category pages for the current roster rather than relying on a static count.

**What is the best order to attempt the Eternal Abyss all towers in?**

Start with entry-tier towers to learn the jump physics, then deliberately move into towers that use whichever mechanic you fail most often. Save standalone towers — the ones placed away from the main cluster — for milestone attempts, since they tend to function as deliberate skill checks.

**Are community tier lists reliable?**

They are useful but not authoritative. Tier rankings are compiled from player experience, and results shift depending on your device, your connection, and your personal strengths. Treat a tier list as a starting filter, not a verdict, and adjust as you learn your own patterns.

**Why is the game so punishing on purpose?**

The game's own description states plainly that it offers little to no handholding and that losing progress is an expected part of the experience. That is a deliberate design choice aimed at players who enjoy mastery through repetition. If that sounds appealing, the difficulty becomes the point rather than an obstacle.
