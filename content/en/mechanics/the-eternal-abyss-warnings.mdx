## Why The Eternal Abyss Warnings Matter Before You Touch a Portal

Every tower in the game sits behind a portal, and almost every portal carries a short warning hint — a word or two that tells you what the tower is like before you commit to it. The community-maintained history for The Eternal Abyss records a March 26 update that gave every tower portal its own custom warning hint, with documented examples such as INCONSISTENT, LOOPFAILY, PUNISHING, JANK, and SCARY. That one change turned the portal ring from a list of names into a menu of expectations.

Why should you care? Because the game is deliberately unforgiving. The [official Roblox listing for The Eternal Abyss](https://www.roblox.com/games/15873244701/The-Eternal-Abyss) describes it as very difficult with little-to-no handholding, and it states outright that losing progress and raging are expected parts of the experience. There is no difficulty slider, no practice mode that removes the sting, and no refund when a long run collapses. The warnings are the closest thing to a difficulty rating you get.

Treat The Eternal Abyss warnings as a contract between you and the designer. When a portal says PUNISHING, it is not being cute — it is telling you that mistakes will be expensive. When it says JANK, it is admitting that the tower leans on engine quirks and uneven collision rather than polished timing. Reading that contract before you enter is free. Ignoring it costs hours.

**Quick context:** the game's wiki page notes that an older page title made it look connected to another tower game, EToH. Community documentation states there is no real affiliation beyond a hidden reference. If you came here looking for that other game's warning system, you are in the wrong place.

## The Warning Hints You'll See on Tower Portals

The documented tags are short on purpose. The Eternal Abyss warnings have to be readable at a glance while you are standing on a portal pad, probably with other players sprinting past you. Each one compresses an entire design philosophy into a single word.

Here is how the most commonly cited hints translate into actual play, based on community reports and player experience:

| Warning hint | Plain-English meaning | What it usually looks like in play | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| INCONSISTENT | The tower changes rules or quality as you climb | Difficulty and design shift between sections; early jumps feel nothing like late ones | Players who adapt fast and don't mind relearning |
| LOOPFAILY | Fails tend to send you back through content you already beat | Repeated segments, long resets, heavy memorization | Patient players with strong short-term memory |
| PUNISHING | Mistakes are expensive | Tight timing, thin margins, brutal fail states | Confident players with solid fundamentals |
| JANK | The tower leans on quirks rather than clean design | Odd hitboxes, physics tricks, unconventional tech | Players who enjoy exploiting engine behavior |
| SCARY | The tone is the challenge | Tension, atmosphere, and unsettling presentation carry the difficulty | Players who stay calm under pressure |

Community documentation makes a point of saying the list is "including but not limited to" those five, which is worth remembering. A portal hint you have never seen before is not a typo — it is a new piece of information, and it deserves the same respect as the tags you already recognize.

## Difficulty Warnings vs. Design Warnings

New players tend to lump every tag together as "hard." That is a mistake. The hints describe different kinds of pressure, and the kind of pressure matters more than the amount.

### Execution pressure: PUNISHING and LOOPFAILY

These two describe how much the tower demands from your hands and your memory. PUNISHING towers test precision — the jump is possible, but the window is narrow. LOOPFAILY towers test endurance and recall, because the cost of a mistake is a long walk back through familiar ground.

If you have limited time in a session, these are the tags that decide whether you finish anything. A PUNISHING tower can eat an entire evening. A LOOPFAILY tower can eat it and give you nothing back.

### Design pressure: INCONSISTENT and JANK

These tags describe the tower itself rather than your skill. INCONSISTENT warns that the quality or logic of the build varies — you cannot settle into a rhythm because the rules keep changing. JANK warns that the tower depends on behavior that was never intended to be precise: odd collision, physics quirks, and tech that works because the engine allows it, not because the designer planned it.

JANK towers are polarizing. Some players find them hilarious and satisfying to solve. Others find them infuriating, because the failure does not feel like their fault. Knowing which camp you are in saves a lot of frustration.

### Atmosphere pressure: SCARY

SCARY is the odd one out. It does not necessarily mean the platforming is harder — it means the experience is designed to make you uncomfortable. Dark sightlines, sudden audio, and a general sense of dread are the tools. The real challenge is staying composed enough to execute jumps you already know how to do.

| Pressure family | Tags | What it tests | Best preparation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Execution | PUNISHING, LOOPFAILY | Timing, consistency, memory | Warm up on easier towers first; keep sessions short |
| Design | INCONSISTENT, JANK | Adaptability, tolerance for ambiguity | Watch community footage; expect tech, not fairness |
| Atmosphere | SCARY | Composure, nerve | Play with sound on; avoid rushing sections |

Here is how those families tend to feel in terms of time, based on player experience rather than official data:

| Tag | Session cost reported by players | Prep worth doing first |
|---|---|---|
| PUNISHING | High — long attempts, frequent restarts | A 10-minute warm-up on cleared content |
| LOOPFAILY | High — resets erase real minutes | Memorize the early sections before pushing deep |
| INCONSISTENT | Medium — constant relearning | Watch footage of the later sections |
| JANK | Variable — depends on how fast you learn the tech | Learn the key trick before attempting the full run |
| SCARY | Medium — nerves, not mechanics | Sound on, no rushing, one section at a time |

## How to Turn The Eternal Abyss Warnings Into a Progression Plan

Warning hints are only useful if they change what you do next. Treating The Eternal Abyss warnings as flavor text is the fastest way to waste an evening. The following routine turns a wall of cryptic labels into an actual session plan.

| Step | What to do | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scan the portal ring and note the hints, not the tower names | You are building a difficulty map, not a to-do list |
| 2 | Sort them into the three pressure families | You can only train one skill at a time |
| 3 | Pick one execution, one design, and one atmosphere tower | Variety keeps you sharp instead of grinding a single weakness |
| 4 | Warm up on something you have already cleared | Confidence and timing carry over |
| 5 | Set a stop rule — three collapses on one section, then switch | Prevents a single tag from ending your whole session |
| 6 | Log which hint predicted your experience accurately | Your own data beats anyone else's tier list |

Two practical notes. First, a hint describes the tower, not your readiness — a PUNISHING tower can still be the right pick if your fundamentals feel sharp that day. Second, warnings do not replace broader community tier discussions; they are a first filter, and the tier list is the second.

The main hub area is worth studying for the same reason. [Community wiki documentation](https://pom2.fandom.com/wiki/The_Eternal_Abyss) lists 42 towers in the area and notes that only one of them is marked Non-Soul Crushing. In practical terms, the portal ring is stacked heavily toward demanding content, which makes the warning hints even more valuable as a sorting tool.

## Common Mistakes Players Make With Portal Warnings

| Mistake | Why it costs you | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Treating every hint as "just hard" | You train the wrong skill and blame yourself for the wrong reason | Identify the pressure family first |
| Ignoring unfamiliar tags | You enter blind and learn the meaning the expensive way | Ask in the community or watch a clip before entering |
| Chasing the scariest-sounding tag for clout | SCARY and JANK describe design, not prestige | Match the tag to what you actually enjoy |
| Restarting instantly after a collapse | Tilt turns a PUNISHING tower into an impossible one | Take a 60-second break between serious attempts |
| Assuming a tag is permanent | Towers get revised over time, and hints can change | Re-check the portal after major updates |

One more caution: do not read the hints as a ranking. A SCARY tower is not "above" a JANK tower. They sit on different axes, and comparing them directly is like ranking a test of balance against a test of memory. Both are hard — they are just hard in ways that reward different players.

That is also why two people can look at the same portal and disagree completely. The tag is objective; your reaction to it is not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are The Eternal Abyss warnings exactly?**
They are short hint labels attached to tower portals that describe what a tower is like before you enter. Documented examples include INCONSISTENT, LOOPFAILY, PUNISHING, JANK, and SCARY, and the community notes the list is not limited to those five.

**Is there an official difficulty rating besides the warnings?**
Not in the form of a numbered scale. The game presents itself as very difficult with minimal handholding, so the portal hints function as the closest thing to a built-in difficulty indicator. Anything more granular comes from community discussion.

**Is The Eternal Abyss connected to EToH?**
No. Community documentation states that despite an older page title that made it look related, the game has no affiliation with EToH beyond a hidden reference.

**Do the warning hints mean a tower is badly made?**
No. They describe design intent and pressure type, not quality. A JANK tower can be a favorite among players who enjoy engine quirks, and an INCONSISTENT tower can be loved precisely because it keeps changing on you.
