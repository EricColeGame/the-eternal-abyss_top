## Which In-Game Stats Actually Measure Progress

Not every number on a leaderboard means the same thing. Some measure volume, some measure skill, and some just measure how long you've been grinding. Community history notes that an all-jumps leaderboard stat was added during development, and that the place version became visible at the bottom of the menu — a small quality-of-life change that makes stat comparisons far more trustworthy.

| Stat | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Towers completed | Total towers fully cleared | The headline number most players compare |
| Steeples and Citadels cleared | Side-content completion | Separates completionists from tower-only climbers |
| Jumps completed | Raw movement volume | Measures grinding volume, not skill ceiling |
| All-jumps leaderboard | Cumulative jump total | A community-tracked ranking for grind consistency |
| Place version | Current build of the game | Confirms you're comparing stats on the same patch |
| Difficulty ratings | Community-voted difficulty per structure | Explains why one clear can take far longer than another |

Without a visible place version, comparing your progress to someone else's from six months ago is nearly meaningless. The game changes; your stats should be read against the version you actually played.

### Reading difficulty honestly

A tower rated near the top of the scale isn't just "harder." It usually means longer, more precise, and far less forgiving of a single mistake. In a game where losing progress is an acknowledged part of the design, a difficulty rating functions as a risk assessment: how much time are you willing to lose on this attempt?

That framing changes how you should read your own numbers. A failed attempt on an extremely high-rated tower isn't a bad stat — it's expected data.

## How to Track the Eternal Abyss Stats That Matter Most

Tracking is the difference between "I feel like I'm improving" and "I know I'm improving." Follow a repeatable workflow instead of eyeballing your progress.

| Step | Action | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Screenshot your stats before each session | Creates a baseline you can compare against |
| 2 | Log which tower you attempted and how far you got | Identifies the specific sections costing you runs |
| 3 | Record session length | Reveals fatigue-driven failures |
| 4 | Re-check the place version after updates | Prevents comparing stats across patches |
| 5 | Review weekly, not daily | Progress in this genre is lumpy, not linear |

A few habits that pay off over a full progression run:

- Track your **best attempt** per tower, not just completions.
- Count failures per session — if the number climbs, stop and come back fresh.
- Use the in-game menu stat display as your primary source of truth.
- Note which side content (Steeples, Mini Tower) you've cleared so you don't double-count progress.
