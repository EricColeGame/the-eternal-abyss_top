Few Roblox tower games are as blunt about their cruelty as The Eternal Abyss. It hands you almost nothing — no checkpoint charity, no forgiving timers — and fully expects you to lose an hour of progress before you learn a single jump. That is exactly why the eternal abyss difficulty ranking matters: it tells you which tower is a reasonable next step and which one will quietly delete your evening.

This guide walks through the community's difficulty scale, shows where each tier sits, and lays out a progression path you can actually follow. Whether you are chasing your first Easy clear or staring up at a Nil-tier monster, the eternal abyss difficulty ranking below will help you pick a fight you can win.

## How the Difficulty Scale Works in The Eternal Abyss

The Eternal Abyss sorts its towers with a difficulty icon system — the same broad convention used across Roblox's tower-climbing genre. Each tower carries an icon that signals roughly how much precision, timing, and endurance it demands before you even enter it.

According to community-maintained wiki pages, the area's towers span from **Easy** at the bottom all the way up to **Nil** at the top, though only towers up through **Unreal** are currently playable. That distinction matters: some of the highest-rated icons exist as placeholders or future content, so a scary-looking chart entry does not always mean a clearable challenge today.

The official game listing reinforces the tone. It describes The Eternal Abyss as a very difficult game with little-to-no handholding, and warns that losing progress and getting frustrated are simply part of the experience. In other words, the difficulty ranking is not decoration — it is the map.

### The three questions every icon answers

| Question | What it tells you | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| How precise are the jumps? | Whether you need pixel-perfect positioning | Determines if you can brute-force or must memorize |
| How long is the run? | Total floors and section length | Long towers punish mistakes far more harshly |
| Does it wipe progress? | Soul-crushing vs. non-soul-crushing | One death can cost an entire session |

## The Eternal Abyss Difficulty Ranking, Tier by Tier

Below is a practical ordering of the difficulty bands you will encounter, based on community data and player experience. Treat it as a ladder: each band assumes you have already comfortably cleared the one beneath it.

| Rank | Difficulty band | What it demands | Best suited for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy | Basic jumps, generous timing | First-time players |
| 2 | Medium / Hard | Consistent movement, light memorization | Players with a few clears |
| 3 | Difficult / Challenging | Precision jumps, longer sections | Intermediate climbers |
| 4 | Intense / Remorseless | Tight timing, punishing resets | Experienced players |
| 5 | Insane | Near-flawless execution | Dedicated grinders |
| 6 | Extreme | Long, technical, unforgiving | Veterans only |
| 7 | Terrifying | Sustained precision over many floors | Top-tier players |
| 8 | Unreal | The current practical ceiling | The best of the best |
| 9 | Nil | Listed but effectively out of reach | Nobody — yet |

### Notes on the top of the chart

**Insane** is where most players hit their first real wall. A good example is the Tower of Difficulty Chart, which community pages describe as an Insane-difficulty tower whose gimmick is that each floor escalates in difficulty as you climb. That design philosophy — ramp up pressure floor by floor — shows up again and again as you move up the eternal abyss difficulty ranking.

**Terrifying** and **Unreal** are endurance tests. At this level, the challenge is rarely a single hard jump; it is stringing together dozens of hard jumps without a mistake. **Nil** sits at the very top of the scale as an aspirational icon rather than a realistic target.

Practically speaking, that changes how you should approach the eternal abyss difficulty ranking. A tower two tiers above your comfort zone is not just harder — it is harder *and* unforgiving about failure.

## A Practical Progression Path

Here is a milestone-based route that matches the ranking above. Timings are rough community estimates, not official figures — individual skill varies enormously.

| Stage | Target difficulty | Focus skill | Rough time investment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy | Movement basics, camera control | A few hours |
| 2 | Medium–Hard | Consistent jump timing | 5–15 hours |
| 3 | Difficult–Challenging | Precision and memorization | 20–40 hours |
| 4 | Intense–Remorseless | Recovering from mistakes mid-run | 40–80 hours |
| 5 | Insane | Full-run consistency | 80+ hours |
| 6 | Extreme and above | Endurance and mental reset | Long-term project |

### How to use this path

- **Clear everything in your current tier before jumping up.** Skipping bands is the fastest way to burn out.
- **Track your death floors.** If you consistently die on floor 6 of a tower, that is your practice target, not the whole tower.
- **Rotate between two towers.** Grinding one brutal tower for six hours straight is how sessions end in frustration.
- **Save the non-soul-crushing tower for a bad day.** It is the one clear in the area that will not punish you.

## Tips to Climb Higher Without Rage-Quitting

The game's own store page warns that losing progress and raging are expected parts of the experience. That is not a bug — it is the design. Your job is to manage it.

| Common mistake | Better approach |
|---|---|
| Repeating a failed section immediately | Take a 5-minute break, then retry |
| Playing past your tier for bragging rights | Drop one tier, rebuild confidence |
| Ignoring the difficulty icon | Use the icon as a hard filter |
| Practicing only the hardest jump | Practice the *transition* into it |
| Grinding alone indefinitely | Compare notes with other players |

Community reports consistently show that players who pace themselves progress faster over weeks than players who binge. The eternal abyss difficulty ranking is a planning tool — use it to schedule your attempts, not to measure your worth.

## FAQ

**What is the hardest difficulty in The Eternal Abyss?**
Nil sits at the top of the community scale, above Unreal. However, wiki data indicates that only towers up through Unreal are currently playable, so Nil functions as an aspirational icon for now.

**How many towers are in The Eternal Abyss?**
Community wiki pages list 42 towers in the main area, plus one Mini Tower, two Steeples, and two Citadels. Only one of those towers is non-soul-crushing.

**Is the eternal abyss difficulty ranking official?**
No. It is built from community wiki data and player experience. The game's own listing confirms the general tone — very difficult, minimal handholding — but the tier ordering itself comes from the player base.

**Should I start with Insane towers if I am confident?**
You can, but expect a wall. Insane-tier towers like the Tower of Difficulty Chart escalate floor by floor, and with almost no non-soul-crushing content in the area, a single mistake usually means starting over. Build a base of mid-tier clears first.
