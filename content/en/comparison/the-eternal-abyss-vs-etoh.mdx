## Why the "The Eternal Abyss vs EToH" Question Keeps Coming Up

Search for The Eternal Abyss vs EToH and you land in one of the messiest naming disputes in the Roblox tower-climbing scene. The two names get swapped constantly — in Discord servers, tier-list comments, and even in the game's own page history — which leaves new players unsure whether they're looking at one game, two games, or a spin-off. Sorting out The Eternal Abyss vs EToH matters because the answer changes what you're signing up for: a separate, punishingly difficult tower game with its own areas and progression, or the long-running Juke's Towers of Hell lineage that most veterans mean when they type "EToH."

Here's the short version before the details: **The Eternal Abyss is its own game.** According to the game's community wiki, its Roblox page was formerly titled "EToH: The Eternal Abyss," but the developers state it has no affiliation with EToH whatsoever beyond a hidden portal. That single line of page history is responsible for most of the confusion you'll see online.

This guide breaks down both sides of The Eternal Abyss vs EToH comparison, explains the terminology that gets mixed up (towers, areas, soul crushing), and helps you decide which one to actually grind first.

## The Eternal Abyss at a Glance

The Eternal Abyss — often abbreviated **TEA** — is a Roblox tower-climbing game whose main area shares its name. Per the game's community wiki, that starting area contains **42 towers**, and only one of them is a non-soul-crushing tower, which tells you almost everything about the intended audience.

The official game page describes it as "a very difficult game with little-to-no handholding," and mentions a long-term goal of 50 towers in each area. In practice, that means no tutorializing, no gentle difficulty ramp, and no forgiveness for sloppy movement.

## EToH at a Glance: What the Name Actually Refers To

EToH is community shorthand that circulates around the **Juke's Towers of Hell** family of Roblox obby games. It's the name that veteran players use when they talk about the established tower-climbing meta — the difficulty charts, the tier lists, the tower rankings, and the shared vocabulary of "steeple," "citadel," and "soul crushing."

Two things make EToH hard to pin down for newcomers:

- **The abbreviation gets applied loosely.** Community tools and tier lists use "EToH" as an umbrella label, so the exact boundary of what counts as EToH varies depending on who's talking.
- **The name travels.** Because "EToH" is recognizable, unrelated projects have borrowed it in their titles, which is exactly how The Eternal Abyss ended up with a mismatched page name.

The practical takeaway: when someone says "EToH," they usually mean the long-running Juke's Towers of Hell lineage and its surrounding community infrastructure — not The Eternal Abyss.

## Progression, Difficulty, and Which Game Fits You

Difficulty is the whole personality of both games, but they distribute it differently. EToH's ecosystem spans an enormous range, so a brand-new player can find a foothold. The Eternal Abyss, by contrast, opens with a main area where nearly everything demands soul-crushing-level commitment — community reports describe it as a game that assumes you already know how to play.

### Actionable tips before you start either one

- **Learn the completion rules first.** Soul crushing usually means one continuous session with no quitting out. If you don't know the rule before you start a tower, you'll lose the run on a technicality.
- **Warm up elsewhere.** If your hardest clear is a mid-tier tower in another Roblox obby game, The Eternal Abyss's main area will flatten you.
- **Bookmark the community wiki.** Both scenes rely on player-written documentation far more than in-game tutorials.
- **Record your runs.** Frame data and death logs are the fastest way to find which jump in a tower is actually killing you.
- **Pick one tower and finish it.** Progress in both games comes from completed towers, not from scouting twenty of them.

## FAQ

**Is The Eternal Abyss part of EToH?**
No. According to the game's community wiki, The Eternal Abyss has no affiliation with EToH other than a hidden portal. The confusion traces back to its Roblox page formerly being titled "EToH: The Eternal Abyss," which players reasonably read as a connection.

**Why does The Eternal Abyss vs EToH come up so often in community spaces?**
Because the abbreviations overlap and the page history muddied the water. Players searching for EToH content sometimes land on The Eternal Abyss instead, and vice versa, so the comparison gets re-litigated constantly in Discord servers and tier-list comment sections.

**How many towers does The Eternal Abyss have?**
Community sources report 42 towers in the main area that shares the game's name, with only one of them being non-soul-crushing. The game's stated long-term target is 50 towers per area, so expect that number to grow over time.

**Which one should a beginner play?**
Start with the EToH / Juke's Towers of Hell lineage if you're new. Its difficulty range is broader and its documentation is deeper. The Eternal Abyss is described by its own page as very difficult with little-to-no handholding, which makes it a rough first stop unless you already have serious obby experience.
